Naming is a first encounter, not a complete lesson

The sampled pages pair Maithili and English animal names, including zebra, giraffe and monkey. The restrained amount of text makes recognition the central task. The format can support movement between a spoken name and a pictured creature without requiring a child to follow a complicated narrative.

Its limit is also its design: a name does not explain habitat, behaviour or the differences among animals. That need not be a defect in a first vocabulary book, provided adult use does not demand knowledge the page never offers. The translation's value lies in placing Maithili within the act of naming alongside English. The original writers and image makers remain separately credited in the source. This note assesses selected text-bearing pages, not the quality of every illustration or a complete learning sequence.
