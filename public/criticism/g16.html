Which difficulties should an adaptation keep?

The introductory account describes an adaptation that retains the original's story, characters, symbols and chapter sequence while simplifying its presentation. The sampled dialogue remains interested in social relationships, and a passage about trust uses a network image rather than treating connection as a purely technical matter. The adaptation therefore preserves conceptual ambitions alongside its narrative inheritance.

That is more demanding than merely shortening sentences. An adolescent reader still needs manageable units, clear transitions and enough contextual support to follow a large fictional world. At 773 PDF pages, accessibility cannot be inferred from the edition label alone. A useful comparison would examine one scene in both versions: what background disappears, what becomes explicit, and whether ambiguity survives. This assessment concerns sampled passages and the edition's stated method; it is not a finding from testing with young readers.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

Which difficulty does the adaptation remove, and which difficulty does it preserve as part of the reader's discovery?
