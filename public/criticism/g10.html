Read the polemic against the textual evidence

The contents place an extended polemical opening beside studies of Gharari, Gamwali, Bhamati and Asmita. This arrangement makes criticism an intervention in literary authority as well as an encounter with individual works. The sampled use of genealogical material and discussion of dramatic action shows how far the argument ranges beyond a purely formal vocabulary.

Such context can expose the conditions under which reputations are made. It cannot, by itself, determine a play's dramatic effectiveness or a narrative's achievement. The useful critical distinction is between evidence about a writer's position and evidence about the writing. Read the work-specific chapters for moments when pace, conflict or voice complicate the polemic. The collection is strongest as an invitation to test inherited evaluations, provided its own evaluations remain equally open to testing.
