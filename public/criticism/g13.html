What does the epilogue settle?

The paired organisation places each Maithili play beside an English stage adaptation. This is a significant description of method: an adaptation has to work through bodies, timing and audible speech, not simply transfer sentences. A sampled freeze involving workers, an engineer and a contractor points to the stage's power to organise a social relationship visually.

The English epilogue sampled from Sankarshan, by contrast, articulates an explicit moral interpretation. That contrast identifies a useful critical tension between what a scene lets an audience infer and what a closing voice tells it to conclude. Neither strategy is automatically superior; their effects depend on performance. Read the two versions with pauses and entrances in mind. The present note assesses selected written passages, not a staged production or a line-by-line translation comparison.
