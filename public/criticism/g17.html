Accessibility becomes a question inside the story

A sampled exchange gives this adaptation a telling moment: the abstract language of human dignity is challenged by a request to say the same thing in a worker's language. The speaker's difficulty makes communication part of the scene's ethical problem. That is a promising principle for an adaptation: an idea should not count as accessible merely because it has been named.

The retained chapter architecture and the substantial length mean that a children's edition still asks for sustained attention. Its effectiveness will depend on how individual episodes support understanding, not just on a simplified vocabulary. Read the adaptation for situations in which explanation becomes action or dialogue. This note does not assume that all difficult themes have been removed, nor does the children's label establish suitability for every age; those questions need complete reading and attention to the particular reader.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

Does the scene find a concrete way to express dignity after exposing the limits of an abstract phrase?
