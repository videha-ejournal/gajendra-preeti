Living beside the river

The opening identifies Mishra's river books as the subject of study, while the sampled discussion links rehabilitation to everyday life and considers the cultural and ecological dimensions of a river. Gajendra Thakur's role here is that of a reader and critic of this writing. The river emerges as an inhabited environment whose meaning cannot be exhausted by a single measure of flood control.

That perspective gives the criticism a strong ethical centre: relief, displacement and ways of living with water belong within the same conversation. The analytical challenge is to keep testimony, historical explanation and technical claims distinct while showing their relationship. A literary appreciation can reveal whose experience a policy narrative overlooks; it cannot substitute for independent hydrological verification. Read for the form through which local experience becomes evidence and for the limits the critic places on generalisation.
