First, the verse to Ganesha was sung… — Editorial

Ashish Anchinhaar

Anchinhaar explains the volume’s three sections, its mixture of older and newly commissioned writing, and its folk-song framework. He asks readers to recognize Nagendra Kumar Jha and Panjikar Vidyanand Jha in jointly edited work, even where articles omit them. He identifies subjects still awaiting assessment and invites corrections. His account of editorial vision, spelling variation and linked references clarifies how this particular collection was assembled.

Turning the Pages, the Village’s Director: K.N.T.

Siyaram Jha ‘Saras’

Saras remembers Kripanand Thakur through the institutions of village life: tutoring, the Rambahadur public library, theatre, football and engineering service. Personal recollections show how collective effort widened educational and cultural opportunities, and how factional conflict could undo them. The concluding account of professional integrity connects this remembered example with Gajendra and Preeti’s work. It is a participant’s biographical memoir, not an independently verified institutional history.

Gonu Jha and Other Maithili Picture Stories

Prof. Veena Thakur

Thakur reads the interplay of words, lines and colour as a means of transmitting Mithila’s folk culture. Moving through the collection’s heroes and traditions, she argues that picture narrative expands Mithila painting beyond familiar ceremonial subjects. Her discussion links artistic change with cultural continuity and emphasizes women’s role in preserving and renewing folk art.

Gajendra Thakur’s Contribution to Panji Literature

Binayanand Jha

Jha welcomes the jointly edited Panji volumes while testing their genealogical readings against other records. Comparisons of Mandar lineages expose variant names, missing generations and uncertain identifications; a proposed reconciliation remains provisional. He asks for better indexes of ancestral villages and branches and further research on royal genealogies. The essay demonstrates why making manuscripts available and establishing a reliable lineage are different scholarly tasks.

What is Panji Prabandh?

Jagdish Chandra Thakur ‘Anil’

Anil introduces Panji terminology, record types, genealogists and the relationship between marriage documentation and social hierarchy. He emphasizes the difficulty posed by unfamiliar scripts and the value of transliteration for readers. Crucially, he says that many pages remain unread: this is an introductory response during an unfinished reading, not a comprehensive assessment. The historical rules described in the essay should be distinguished from present-day endorsement of them.

Dushan Panji: Gajendra Thakur’s Universal Contribution to Maithili Culture

Dr. Kailash Kumar Mishra

Mishra reads Panji as a record of social arrangements whose exclusions require examination. Dushan records, including accounts of marriages outside prescribed boundaries, complicate idealized narratives of purity. He proposes comparing manuscripts and transcriptions, training readers in Tirhuta and extending attention to women and other communities. The long discussion is a sociological research agenda as much as a book response; he cautions against excessive scientific claims and acknowledges limits to his assessment.

Gajendra Thakur: Preserver of Mithila’s Heritage

Dr. Shiv Kumar Mishra

Mishra sets manuscript digitization against the practical difficulties of conservation: reluctant owners, limited cooperation and the declining ability to read older scripts. He values Gajendra Thakur’s preservation work while calling for script training and sustained scholarly attention. A preserved image alone cannot ensure that a manuscript’s contents remain accessible to future readers.

Mithila Mihir and Videha Web Magazine

Lakshman Jha ‘Sagar’
