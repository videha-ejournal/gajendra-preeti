The intermediate language belongs in the story

The publisher's contents identify material from several regional languages and make English intermediaries visible in the translation route. That information is more than a bibliographic detail. A translation made through another translation encounters choices already made about rhythm, ambiguity and cultural explanation.

Transparency about the route lets readers ask a more precise question than whether the final text feels fluent. Which distinctions may have passed through English, and which can the Maithili version reopen or explain? The anthology's opportunity is to make circulation across languages available to readers without hiding the labour and mediation involved. This is a publisher-based critical introduction; no claim of line-by-line fidelity is made. A fuller assessment would compare a selected original, its intermediary and the Maithili text together.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

Which feature of a passage would require consulting the original language rather than only its English intermediary?
