A slow opening can earn its length

The contents range across poetry, fiction and translated works. In the sampled criticism, a discussion of Goliya weighs the length of an opening against emotional investment, while the treatment of Modiain attends to oral narration and questioning. These are useful distinctions: pace in a novel is not the same problem as concentration in a poem or the force of a spoken narrative.

The volume's comparative opportunity is to let one genre make the expectations of another visible. Its criticism should therefore be judged by how closely an evaluative term fits the passage under discussion. The presence of translated works also requires careful attribution: Lal's role as translator must not erase the original author. The sampled arguments reward a reader who treats criticism as a reasoned account of effects, rather than as a verdict detached from their production.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

When is a slow opening an obstacle, and when does it create the emotional investment a later scene needs?
