A record of lineage is also a record of selection

The publisher presents a multi-volume investigation of Panji material through genealogical, social and historical questions. Such records can preserve relationships across time, but they also embody rules about whose relationships were worth recording. The advertised attention to women's status and forms of exclusion makes those boundaries part of the proposed subject.

The central critical distinction is between documentary genealogy and genetic evidence. Language about mapping a genome should not be read as proof of DNA sequencing unless the work actually supplies that evidence. Nor can records of a particular community automatically represent an entire region. This publisher-based introduction identifies those methodological tests without claiming to have audited the full series. A persuasive reading must make provenance, transcription choices, missing people and uncertainty visible alongside reconstructed lineages.
