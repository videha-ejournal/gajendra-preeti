Three lengths, three kinds of attention

The title material presents a collection of long, short and very short fiction, and the inspected contents and spreads make those changes of scale visible. Compact pieces share the volume with more extended prose; dialogue, section breaks and an English passage near the end add further variation to the reading rhythm. The collection offers an opportunity to compare what a story can withhold at different lengths.

Brevity is not automatically intensity, just as length is not automatically depth. A short piece may depend on a decisive turn, while an extended one can allow a social situation to accumulate through encounter and speech. A full judgement would require following those effects through complete stories. Because text extraction from this edition is unreliable, this introduction confines itself to the visually inspected structure and pages rather than inventing plot summaries or a verdict on the entire collection.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

What kind of development becomes possible in the long form that the shortest pieces must replace with implication?
