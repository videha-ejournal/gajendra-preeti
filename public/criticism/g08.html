Thirteen units, several kinds of reading

The contents bring together dramatic, narrative and satirical material, including Shakuntala, Agamadambara and Bhagavadajjukam within a thirteen-unit compilation. The sampled moral criticism of greed and the more ornate narrative passage demonstrate substantially different reading demands. The collection's attraction lies in enabling movement across forms within one resource.

That convenience can also conceal differences of period, genre, authorship and tone. A sharp satirical utterance and an elaborate narrative description should not be evaluated by the same standard of speed or directness. The editorial task is therefore more than accumulation: introductions, attribution and clear boundaries help each work retain its particularity. Read this as a translation anthology whose parts invite comparison, not as a single continuous original work by its compiler.
