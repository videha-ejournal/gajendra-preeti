What makes a question difficult?

The sampled material ranges from questions about a story's form and a translation's language to details of presentation. It turns the collected archive into a large bank of retrievable observations. That can support revisiting material which might otherwise remain buried in extensive volumes.

The critical issue is the relationship between quantity and discrimination. Some sampled alternatives are very general, which can make recognition easier than the apparent scale suggests. A useful question bank needs a balance of factual recall, source location and interpretive comparison, with clear explanations where an answer is arguable. This note does not verify the advertised total or every answer; it assesses selected examples. Readers can strengthen the resource by treating each answer as a reason to reopen a source, rather than the end of the encounter.
