Why begin with a mistake?

The shell-and-silver example in the sampled opening makes an abstract problem tangible: a mistaken identification requires an account of perception, memory and judgement. The organisation around perception, inference, comparison and word-based knowledge gives the edition a route through that complexity. Error is not an incidental failure here; it is a way to separate operations that ordinary experience runs together.

The pedagogical challenge is to keep these distinctions available over a large work. A term that seems transparent in an opening example may carry a narrower technical sense later. Readers should trace the same distinction across an example and a formal discussion, using the English explanatory material as an aid rather than a replacement for the argument. This is an assessment of sampled organisation and explanation, not a verified comparison with a Sanskrit critical edition.
