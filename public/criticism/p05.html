Preservation makes a source available, not self-explanatory

Videha credits Preeti Thakur with compiling, scanning and cataloguing the original Tirhuta palm-leaf Panji records. This is an archival contribution, so the appropriate critical question differs from that for an authored story. The work makes records available for later reading; its achievement depends on maintaining the relationship between a digital image and the material record it represents.

Availability alone cannot resolve transcription, dating or the social limits of the record. A useful catalogue needs stable identifiers and enough provenance to support comparison, while researchers must distinguish what a manuscript states from what they infer. This introduction is based on the public catalogue; the complete manuscript collection was not inspected. It therefore does not certify individual lineages or the accuracy of every catalogue entry. The archive deserves attention as infrastructure for inquiry, with its gaps kept visible.
