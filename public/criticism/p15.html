Repetition makes a difference noticeable

The sampled pages introduce animals through a repeated naming pattern before distinguishing the lion's food from that of the other animals. The turn from common action to difference gives the sequence a basic comparative structure: eating connects the group, while what is eaten separates its members.

As an introductory picture book, that pattern can support observation and questions. It should not be treated as a comprehensive account of animal diets. The sampled summary groups the other animals as plant eaters, a simplification that a fuller discussion would need to qualify rather than universalise. The literary effect of the contrast and the scientific completeness of the explanation are separate judgements. Preeti Thakur's role is translation; this note addresses selected narrative text, not every page or the source contributors' wider work.
