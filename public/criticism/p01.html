The spoken tale finds a visible rhythm

The inspected pages use stacked panels, caption strips and speech balloons to turn narrative into a sequence of encounters. Bright fields of colour separate episodes, while village interiors, trees and groups of listeners give the stories a recognisable social space. The Gonu Jha material belongs here to a larger picture-story collection, not merely an illustrated title page.

The format's strength is its division of labour: captions carry transitions, while gesture and dialogue can carry a joke, an exchange or a change in power. Its critical tension is density. Where both caption and balloon explain an action already visible, the image has less room to invite inference. The sampled pages support an assessment of visual narration, not a claim that every story has been read. This work is best approached with attention to what becomes intelligible between panels as well as within the words.
