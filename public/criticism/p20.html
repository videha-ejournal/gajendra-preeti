An inherited text enters a different reading form

The inspected edition identifies Preeti Thakur's children's presentation and uses sequential images to stage encounters, including courtly exchanges and journey scenes. That form changes how a reader meets an inherited work: situations become visible before their significance has to be extracted from continuous prose. The source title and the adapter's contribution should both remain in view.

The critical question is what adaptation does with inherited judgements. A children's presentation can make a scene intelligible without requiring the reader to accept every social assumption carried by its source. Pictures, captions and omitted explanation all participate in that decision. Because parts of the digital edition have unreliable text extraction, this note confines itself to visually inspected pages and format. It does not certify fidelity to a Sanskrit source, endorse introductory historical hypotheses, or claim a complete analysis of every story.
