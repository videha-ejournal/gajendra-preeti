Who gets to stand for a literature?

The publisher's visible description foregrounds a dispute over representative accounts of Maithili literature and institutional selection. This positions the work within an argument about the making of a canon: who is included, which institutions confer authority, and which traditions are presented as marginal. The Samagra lists a multi-part project, so the catalogue entry concerns a series rather than one short essay.

The critical opportunity is to widen the archive on which literary history rests. Its necessary discipline is to apply the same evidentiary standard to the alternative account that it asks of established ones. Accusations and counterclaims in a publisher description remain attributed positions, not facts independently established by this page. A full assessment would compare the series' cited works, exclusions and interpretations. This introduction is limited to the public description and series listing.
