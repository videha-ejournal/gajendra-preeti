Colour becomes the trace of an encounter

A white bear's movement through the inspected pages gathers colours through contact with the surrounding world. Yellow ears, a watermelon encounter and a green back turn colour naming into a record of experience. The changing body provides continuity across the sequence, so each new colour has a narrative context.

The strongest feature is that the vocabulary does not float free of action. Readers can ask where a colour came from and use the picture to reconstruct an encounter. Repeated bilingual phrases provide support, while the image carries more detail than the phrase alone. This is an assessment of selected visual pages, not a measured claim about how children learn colours. Preeti Thakur's translation should be credited alongside, rather than in place of, the original writer and illustrator listed by the book.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

Can you reconstruct the bear's recent experience by looking only at the colours it carries?
