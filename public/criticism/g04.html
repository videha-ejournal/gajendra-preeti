Whose argument are we reading?

This edition presents philosophical commentary through another language, bringing the layered discussion associated with Bhamati into Maithili. The sampled treatment of conscious and unconscious causation, the example involving grammatical knowledge, and the discussion of self and misidentification show why a reader needs more than a vocabulary list. Each proposition receives its meaning from the objection or distinction to which it responds.

The edition's opportunity is to make that argumentative movement readable without flattening it into a set of spiritual conclusions. Its difficulty is equally structural: readers must distinguish the underlying text, the commentator's reasoning and the translator's explanation. Read a short unit twice, first for its conclusion and then for the alternatives it excludes. This approach values the translation as an opening into study while leaving Sanskrit fidelity and textual variants to a separate, source-by-source comparison.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

Can you identify whose voice is speaking at each stage of a single objection and reply?
