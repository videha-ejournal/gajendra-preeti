A silent page asks for sound

The repeated phrase naming the sound of an instrument organises the sampled pages around a drum, a hand guitar and a harp. In a printed book, that phrase creates a productive absence: the sound itself is not on the page. Reading can therefore become imitation, comparison or an invitation to listen elsewhere.

The translation also makes instrument naming a cultural decision. A familiar local term can offer an entry point, but a reader should still ask whether the pictured instrument and the chosen name refer to the same object or only a rough analogue. That is a question for close comparison, not an error established by this note. The sampled text suggests a useful read-aloud resource when supplemented by actual listening. It cannot demonstrate auditory recognition through printed repetition alone.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

How would the reading change if each page were followed by a short recording of the named instrument?
