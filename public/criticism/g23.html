Autoplay and study pull attention differently

The documentary page organises 904 source frames with search, contents, study and documentary controls, as well as options for listening and text size. These affordances recognise that a reader may want either a guided sequence or a return to a particular concept. Searchable text can make a large teaching resource more approachable than a presentation that must be traversed slide by slide.

The critical tension lies between the pace of autoplay and the pauses that interpretation requires. A well-marked study route lets the reader interrupt, compare and revisit; a documentary sequence can supply momentum but also encourage acceptance of its framing. This note considers the published controls and structure, without claiming a usability study, testing speech output, or verifying every frame against the novel. Treat the documentary as an interpretation with a designed pace, not as transparent access to the source work.
