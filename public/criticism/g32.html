Comparison works best when it preserves disagreement

The contents establish a broad conversation between Indian and Western philosophical material. The sampled discussion of pragmatism attends to inquiry and testing, while English material on the limits of knowledge distinguishes doubt from unsupported belief. These are promising points of contact because they concern operations of thought rather than merely matching famous names.

A comparative resource becomes less useful when a resemblance is treated as proof of identity or historical influence. The productive question is what remains different after a parallel has been drawn: the problem being addressed, the accepted evidence, or the purpose of the argument. This note assesses selected explanations and the comparative architecture. It does not certify every account of a school or every proposed connection across the volume.

A QUESTION TO CARRY BACK

After identifying a parallel, what disagreement still prevents the two positions from becoming interchangeable?
