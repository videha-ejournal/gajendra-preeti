The comparison must survive its own breadth

The advertised contents move through analytic and continental philosophy, including language, meaning, speech acts, structuralism and deconstruction, while introducing comparisons with Nyaya. Such a structure can help readers see that a question about language may connect to a larger question about knowledge or social life.

Its critical risk is over-compression: a shared word such as meaning does not guarantee a shared philosophical problem. The best comparative account specifies both the resemblance and the point at which the comparison fails. This introduction is based on the publisher's contents rather than the full volume. It does not verify each account of a philosopher or infer a historical connection from thematic proximity. Readers should test one proposed parallel in detail before using the book's broad architecture as a settled map.
