Oral encounters acquire panels and gestures

The sampled stories move through village households, groups, courtly settings and encounters between speakers. Stacked panels, caption bands and speech balloons provide a consistent visual grammar, while strong colour separates episodes. The opening Moti Dai material and later tales show that the collection relies on social exchanges as well as isolated action.

The format makes a spoken tradition available to the eye, but it also fixes one arrangement of bodies, expressions and emphasis. A reader can ask what the picture contributes to a judgement about a speaker before the dialogue has finished. The captions are useful for transitions; where they repeat what is already visible, they may reduce the reader's work of inference. This assessment is based on selected pages and does not turn them into invented summaries of every tale in the collection.
