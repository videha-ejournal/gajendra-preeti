Nav Pankhi — New Wings

Gajendra Thakur · Stories for young readers · 129 PDF pages

The book and its concerns

The supplied contents list twenty teenage stories, ranging from displacement and migration to libraries, floods, science, mobile phones, trees and shared investigation. The opening story explores dimensions through a laboratory conversation: beings confined to a line cannot perceive the possibilities available from another dimension. The scene makes a limit of perception available to imaginative inquiry rather than presenting a scientific demonstration of time travel.

A later sampled passage describes a village exhibition recording flood levels, safe places, drainage routes and remembered experience. Preserving a damaged book becomes inseparable from understanding and using what it contains. The final story’s recovered keys lead the children toward observation, reconstruction and checking before belief. Together, these samples place young people in active relations with evidence, community memory and practical problem-solving.

Displacement in Space and Time

कथा १ — काल-स्थान विस्थापन

Dhaumya uses one- and two-dimensional worlds to teach his five students the limits of perception, then builds Pushpak to test a passage through curved space-time. Ghosha insists on rules against altering the past, and the teacher signs the same agreement as everyone else. He returns from what feels like part of a night to a world roughly a thousand years later, where his extraordinary technology has become familiar. Instead of demanding recognition, he asks for a library and introduces himself as a student of time.

The reversal transforms the discoverer into a learner. Technical daring is accompanied by responsibility, uncertainty and the loss of contemporaries. The scientific vocabulary belongs to speculative fiction; the narrative does not establish the practical possibility of its time-travel apparatus.

Life on Crutches

कथा २ — बैसाखीपर जिनगी

Ratisch and Phool meet after eighteen years while their sons sit an engineering entrance examination. Recollections of school lead into Phool’s account of his brother’s disability, marriage, family grief and resistance to pity. Only when Phool’s own son emerges does Ratisch discover that the boy uses crutches after an accident. The teenager is eager to discuss physics and mathematics, and his father treats that enthusiasm as the important news.

The delayed disclosure exposes the listener’s assumptions. Phool has already described his son accurately through his curiosity and ambitions; the crutches are one aspect of his life, not the identity that must precede every conversation. The story argues for equal opportunity without pretending that physical effort and social barriers disappear.

I Will Not Go Abroad

कथा ३ — हम नै जाएब विदेश

Saurabh has a scholarship for environmental engineering abroad, but a visit to historian Dwijendra makes him reconsider how ambition relates to home. Dwijendra regrets postponing his relationship with his mother until success arrived too late; he explicitly warns against turning his regret into someone else’s rule. Saurabh’s flood-monitoring work then helps alert a vulnerable neighbourhood. He decides to postpone overseas study and develop that local project, while leaving open the possibility of travelling later.

The title’s refusal is a decision for the present, not an argument against learning abroad. The story distinguishes staying through personal judgement from staying out of fear or emotional obligation. Knowledge gains direction when it remains answerable to real problems and continuing relationships.

Away from Home

कथा ४ — प्रवास

Moving from Madhubani to Haldia leaves Nachiket outside the language and friendships of his new school. He befriends Tapas, a skilled maker whose poor confidence on stage has kept his contribution in the background. Their demand for proper credit creates friction with team leader Devadatta. During severe weather, however, a simplified water-level alarm becomes useful to local volunteers, and the rivals combine their work. At the science fair the group receives recognition for local usefulness rather than first prize.

Belonging develops through shared work, reciprocal language learning and fair attribution. The emergency makes practical cooperation more important than ownership of the team. Migration ends its sharpest isolation when other people’s concerns and achievements begin to feel connected to one’s own.

An Exchange of Places

कथा ५ — हराहरी

Anay recognises the quiet student Chandan in a library as the exuberant boy who once befriended him at an examination centre. Their dispositions seem to have exchanged places. Chandan explains how unemployment at home, repeated school changes and evening work reduced his opportunities to speak; Anay recalls how encouragement in public speaking loosened his own fear. Their friendship leads to an open discussion table where listening is permitted and no one is mocked into participation.

The recurring shared samosa connects the two encounters without requiring either young person to return to a fixed former self. Silence may express thought, fatigue, fear or lack of opportunity. A label such as shy or talkative cannot explain the circumstances that produce behaviour, much less determine a whole future.

The Morning Star Beneath the Pond

कथा ६ — पोखरिक तरे भोरुक तारा

Kusum and her friends record a bright point that seems to lie beneath Tarapokhari. Ripples fragment it, and its apparent position changes as observers move. Their teacher separates what they saw from what they assumed, using a lamp reflected in a bowl to prepare further observation. A gap between trees finally reveals the bright object in the sky; the teacher identifies it as Venus. Kusum’s grandmother connects the old story of a star drinking water with earlier ways of recognising dawn.

The investigation corrects an account of physical location without making inherited storytelling worthless. Measurement, repeated visits and changes of viewpoint explain the reflection; oral memory records how a landscape once helped people organise time.

The Village’s Old Library

कथा ७ — गामक पुरान पुस्तकालय

Mayank, Neha and Indraneel investigate a locked building that once served as the village library. Permission, a search through old keys and patient repair open the door. Inside, a lending register reconnects their teacher to a formative astronomy book, while photographs and handwritten materials recover other readers’ histories. Cleaning grows into cataloguing, conservation and a memory collection. The reopened library uses a rotating group of volunteers so that its future does not depend on one caretaker.

The important recovery is circulation, not merely a tidy room. A book passed from the oldest reader to the youngest replaces a ribbon-cutting ceremony. The story makes preservation an ongoing arrangement of access, shared work and institutional continuity.

The Mystery of the Goat’s Bell

कथा ८ — खस्सी घंटीक रहस्य

A bell sounds near the old orchard at night, although the goat Chand is always back in his enclosure by morning. Prachi and her friends compare the sound with physical traces, mark the latch and observe safely with adults. Chand has learned to lift the fastening with his horn. Following him reveals visits to Dulari, who cared for him when he was young. A secure latch and supervised daytime visits preserve the relationship without permitting unsafe wandering.

Explanation proceeds from sound to marks, mechanism and observation. Yet the discovery is also emotional: a frightening rumour conceals an animal’s remembered route to care. Solving the puzzle creates a responsibility to protect that attachment.

The Book Saved from the Flood

कथा ९ — बाढ़िमे बचल पोथी

As floodwater rises, Ishan and his companions help move books with adults, boards, rope and a boat. A slipping foot makes the limit explicit: no book is worth more than a life. After the water recedes, rescue becomes preparation for the next flood. Shelves are raised, important pages copied and responsibilities recorded. The young people use old flood descriptions and residents’ memories to make an exhibition of water levels, safe places and routes.

The damaged red-covered book retains stains rather than returning to an imaginary untouched condition. Preservation includes learning from the surviving record; the rescued archive becomes useful evidence for the community’s future.

The Children’s Science Fair

कथा १० — नेनाक विज्ञान-मेला

Madhav, Meenakshi, Arindam and Saloni build a preliminary water filter and a pedal-powered lamp from familiar materials. Loose wiring, cloudy output and a slipping belt make revision part of the project. They record measurements and failures, explain the limits of energy production, and insist that clearer-looking filtered water is not necessarily safe to drink. Although they do not win first prize, their public explanations receive special recognition and lead to a continuing hands-on science group.

The decisive achievement is making an explanation testable and useful without exaggerating it. Honest limits and accessible demonstrations count for more than decorative novelty or a flawless-looking display.

The Decision about the Mango Tree

कथा ११ — आमक गाछक निर्णय

A road proposal threatens the large mango tree that provides shade and a gathering place. Sugandha and her friends turn attachment into a practical comparison of routes. A surveyor finds their suggested bend worth technical examination, while drainage remains a problem for engineers to resolve. The completed road leaves the tree standing with open soil around its roots. Fruit is shared and new saplings are planted for people who will live beyond the children’s immediate horizon.

The story gives feeling a productive next step: measurements, alternatives and consultation. Saving a tree is not presented as rejecting every road, but as asking whether development can be designed with more care.

A Day without a Mobile Phone

कथा १२ — मोबाइल बिनु एक दिन

A classroom experiment asks students to notice their phone habits. Arrangements that seemed effortless become awkward, but waiting and outdoor play also create opportunities to attend to people and the evening sky. The discussion afterward acknowledges practical difficulties, including transport information and emergencies. Students choose modest rules about meals, notifications, sleep and conversation rather than accepting a universal ban.

The phone remains a useful instrument. The question is who decides when to open it and how much attention to give it. The story’s emphasis falls on deliberate use and personal circumstances, not on treating all technology as an enemy.

The Boy Who Counts Birds

कथा १३ — चिरैयाँ गिननिहार बालक

Madhav records birds with dates, locations and conditions rather than merely collecting their names. With friends, he uses repeated observations to show that a proposed wedding structure would disturb a useful patch of vegetation. A small relocation preserves the shrubbery and hollow tree. An unfamiliar bird later remains unnamed in his notebook: colour, direction and time are recorded without pretending to know its species.

The blank identification is an honest result, not a failure to complete the page. Observation becomes a way to negotiate care for habitat, and friendship widens a solitary habit into shared inquiry.

The Clay Robot

कथा १४ — माटिक रोबोट

Watching his grandfather sow seeds, Nachiketa wonders whether a small machine could ease the work. The grandfather’s question—how will it understand differing soil?—becomes a design challenge. Tests lead the machine to stop before a pit, while discussion with a potter explores the clay casing’s advantages, fragility and repairability. The ending sends the casing back for improvement rather than announcing a finished invention.

The robot gathers several kinds of expertise: field knowledge, pottery, calculation, teaching and failed trials. Local craft is neither decorative folklore nor a universal technical solution; its usefulness must be tested for the particular task.

The Forest Path and the Talk of Home

कथा १५ — जंगलक बाट, घरक गप

A familiar forest path becomes the setting for a discussion about road development. Neeraj, Sumi, Afreen and Lalit compare route lengths, trees, slopes, water flow and access to fields. A small soil experiment illustrates one effect of ground cover, but they explicitly avoid claiming it proves the behaviour of an entire forest. The eventual road follows the field edge, leaving the older footpath quiet; planting and aftercare continue afterward.

The comparison replaces a simple forest-versus-village opposition. Water, soil, birds, fields and homes are already connected. The young people’s strongest contribution is a reasoned alternative accompanied by limits on what their evidence can establish.

A School Built in a Tree

कथा १६ — गाछ पर बनल पाठशाला

Nayan, Megha, Ishan and Reva turn the shade of a peepal tree into an outdoor learning project. A platform creates possibilities but also obligations. Nesting birds require quieter hours and greater distance; younger children need a learning circle below; rain requires renewed inspection before anyone climbs. The project changes in response to each question rather than treating its initial design as final.

Its achievement is not height or novelty. A living school learns to revise itself in relation to birds, the tree, different learners and continuing safety. The exchange between the platform and the ground keeps education connected to the community around it.

The Girl Who Made a Sundial

कथा १७ — सूरुजघड़ी बनौने बालिका

Neha’s interest in shifting shadows leads her to build and repeatedly check a sundial. Clouds interrupt readings, and later measurements do not fall exactly on the old marks. Instead of hiding these variations, she records when the instrument fails and discusses the difference between local solar time and civil clock time. The school keeps the project in its science garden, where seasonal comparison continues.

A grandmother’s experience starts the question; measurement refines it. The device teaches causes and limits as well as numbers. Recording uncertainty becomes part of a successful demonstration rather than a blemish to conceal.

Friends Working to Protect the River

कथा १८ — नदी बचाबयवला मित्र-मण्डली

Meena and her friends notice changes in birds, waste and water near a river channel. They keep dated observations, connect litter patterns with market days and speak with vendors before reducing the problem to blame. Cleaning leads to questions of drainage, waste management, planting and institutional action. When birds and fish are seen again, Meena records the observation rather than announcing that the river has been completely restored.

The work extends beyond a single clean-up or celebratory photograph. The friends recognise themselves as companions of the river, not its owners, and make continued comparison and maintenance part of responsibility.

A Map of the Moonlit Night

कथा १९ — चाँदनी रातिक मानचित्र

Anvi’s group observes the sky at a consistent place and time, recording dates, moonlight, clouds and visible stars. They compare an estimated north with a compass and learn that fewer visible stars need not mean stars have disappeared. Family and boatmen’s recollections are recorded as distinct sources alongside measurements. A later change in the sky does not invalidate an earlier dated map.

The map is an account of conditions, not a timeless picture or a sufficient navigation device. The story combines respect for inherited experience with reproducible observation, and ends by allowing attentive looking to matter even when no measurement is being taken.

The Cheerful Watchman and the Lost Key

कथा २० — हँसमुख पहरुआ आ हरायल कुंजी

Bisesar’s archive-room key disappears during preparations for a school book exhibition. Meena and her friends reconstruct the previous evening’s tasks and inspect the gap in the key ring. A magnetic search fails because the key is brass. Following the movement of a wooden bench eventually reveals its yellow cord caught in a joint. The recovery leads to a stronger ring, clear storage and a borrowing register.

The solution arises from material properties and an ordered account of actions, not suspicion directed at a person. Bisesar’s humour keeps the search humane, while the new arrangements turn one solved mystery into a more reliable shared practice.
