Code Name: Tarharime Gohi

The book and its concerns

The supplied novel foregrounds names, networks and the traces left by changing identities. In the sampled central dialogue, Sunayana notices a repeated pattern; Abhayakanta distinguishes a changeable name from the habits and recurring mistakes that are harder to alter. Identification is consequently presented as an act of comparing behaviour, not simply accepting a label.

The ending joins memory of violence to the possibility of renewed, less visible danger. Nand Aruni looks toward apparently calm water with a changed understanding of what it can conceal. The question is whether a community will recognise the next trace, rather than whether it already knows the next name. This reading is based on selected passages and the supplied contents; it does not claim to resolve every episode of the investigation. The source expressly identifies its characters and events as fictional.

Chapter 0 — Opening the second chest

अध्याय ०

Nand Aruni returns to the archived Khatans case with Gop Kumar, who distinguishes reading events from understanding their recurring rules. A second chest, marked as the later water, contains numbers, accounts and messages in place of the visible bloodshed of the earlier case. The signature Talgo hi introduces a criminal presence whose face remains concealed.

The archive establishes continuity without making the two crimes identical. A dead offender can leave an operational network behind. Nand’s questions supply the reader’s route into the new investigation, while Gop’s commentary makes remembrance an active practice of recognising patterns.

Chapter 1 — A network without a name

अध्याय १ : नाम बिना जाल

A technically gifted labourer’s son repairs a radio and briefly experiences the pleasure of being recognised. After moving to town, he is humiliated and falsely accused at a mobile shop. The anonymous possibilities of the digital world subsequently become a route into organised fraud: he coordinates others while keeping himself out of view.

The contrast between repairing a neighbour’s possession and exploiting strangers gives the transformation its moral weight. Poverty and humiliation explain an injury to self-worth; they do not erase the victims or justify the resulting exploitation. His wish to remain invisible already conflicts with his wish to be acknowledged.

Chapter 2 — A task force in unfamiliar water

अध्याय २ : कार्यदलक नव पानि

The task force formed during the Khatans investigation must learn to read dispersed financial complaints. Sunayana Thakur, initially underestimated by an older colleague, notices a shared habit across apparently separate cases. Abhaykant Jha contributes accumulated experience, while Arunesh Mishra insists that a promising pattern must be connected to evidence about an identifiable person.

Expertise is distributed across generations rather than possessed by a lone detective. Sunayana changes the object of attention from lists of names to repeated methods. The chapter also introduces a discipline that will complicate every apparent breakthrough: an explanatory inference does not by itself settle guilt.

Chapter 3 — Where two waters meet

अध्याय ३ : दू पानिक संगम

Talgo hi needs the proceeds of digital fraud to become usable material wealth. Birju Sah, a surviving financial intermediary from the Khatans network, possesses the older connections that make this possible. Their alliance joins technical reach to established channels of influence and exchange, even as the younger organiser keeps direct personal contact at a distance.

The meeting of generations renews an existing structure of exploitation. The same dependence that expands the criminal’s power also gives investigators a possible connection between an apparently placeless operation and a local history. Continuity is carried through practical relationships, not merely through repeated villainous names.

Chapter 4 — The first knot

अध्याय ४ : पहिल गिट्ठऽ

Sunayana finds a recurring small error across complaints from different places. Arunesh asks for a trace connected to a living participant, and the inquiry reaches Manoj, who allowed his account to be used while facing family debt and hunger. Abhaykant offers tea and acknowledges his vulnerability without declaring him innocent. Manoj describes how he was recruited, leading to the Talgo hi signature.

The chapter differentiates responsibility within a hierarchy of exploitation. Treating the minor participant only as a disposable arrest would leave the organiser untouched. Attention to his circumstances produces testimony, but the investigators still distinguish a useful lead from a completed case.

Chapter 5 — A changed name, a surviving trace

अध्याय ५ : नाम बदलल, निशान बचल

Silence among contacts alerts Talgo hi that the investigation is approaching. He abandons that signature and reappears as Panikaua. Sunayana initially experiences the disappearance as a complete loss, until Abhaykant returns her to her own insight about habitual methods. The same small repeated error appears in complaints associated with the new identity.

Names are compared with changeable clothing, while habitual movement belongs to the body. This metaphor gives continuity a visible form, though the investigators’ confidence remains part of the fiction rather than a general rule for proving identity. Nand begins to understand the earlier warning about criminals returning under new names as a practical demand on collective memory.

Chapter 6 — The dilemma of distance

अध्याय ६ : दूरीक द्वन्द्व

Repeated attempts to follow Panikaua’s online presence leave Sunayana exhausted. Abhaykant redirects attention to the point where digital wealth must acquire a material use. The team calls back Dhirketu Jha, an ageing contact from the earlier investigation who readily admits his inability to read the screen but remembers local financial relationships.

The contrast between new water and old ground reorganises the investigation. Neither technical analysis nor remembered local knowledge is sufficient alone. Dhirketu’s return gives an apparently obsolete observer a precise role, while the narrative makes dependency on other people the limit of the criminal’s imagined independence.

Chapter 7 — The old crocodile’s shadow

अध्याय ७ : बूढ़ गोहिक छाया

Unexpected expenditure on a closed sugar mill, a dharmashala and a pond contract draws attention, although the narrative first concedes that such activities are not inherently criminal. Comparing old and new files reveals familiar arrangements. Dhirketu names Birju Sah. Arunesh resists an immediate arrest: recognition of an old pattern still needs evidence, and premature action could warn the other participant.

The archive can preserve the knowledge that enables renewed crime and the knowledge that exposes it. That double use prevents memory from becoming an uncomplicated virtue. The chapter’s most important restraint is its separation of suspicious association from demonstrated wrongdoing.

Chapter 8 — Wanting to be seen after erasing a name

अध्याय ८ : जे नाम मेटौलक, से देखाइ चाहैत छल

Panikaua directs money towards his mother’s house, the village road and the family of the shopkeeper who once humiliated him. These unnamed gifts allow him to imagine recognition without disclosure. Dhirketu reports the unusual flow of funds, and Sunayana searches ordinary local records for a former child resident. Abhaykant nevertheless warns that the connection remains an inference.

The return to the injured place gives financial movement an emotional geography. Building a house for his mother and staging an answer to an old insult are entangled rather than interchangeable motives. The chapter exposes the contradiction between anonymous power and the need for an audience without turning that psychological reading into sufficient evidence.

Chapter 9 — Inference and evidence

अध्याय ९ : अनुमान आ प्रमाण

Sunayana asks why the team cannot arrest the person whose name and village it has located. Arunesh describes preparation rather than passive waiting: the suspected person must be connected with the alleged act through support that can be examined. Meanwhile Panikaua establishes apparently legitimate businesses and charitable connections and seeks political protection, gradually giving up the anonymity that sheltered him.

The chapter alternates investigative restraint with the suspect’s growing impatience for public standing. Its account of proof belongs to the novel’s ethical vocabulary, not a legal manual. The dramatic question is whether a convincing account of motive and identity can be converted into an accountable finding.

Chapter 10 — The fracture

अध्याय १० : दरार

Birju urges continued invisibility, but Panikaua dismisses the older man’s caution as fear. Their shared business now conceals incompatible aims: survival for one, public recognition for the other. Under mounting pressure Birju withdraws, dismantles connections and disappears from the case. The investigators allow that departure in the hope that the younger organiser’s dependence will become exposed.

Nand’s question about Birju escaping interrupts any easy account of success. The strategy leaves a knowledgeable intermediary beyond the file’s resolution. The chapter therefore asks readers to remember an unresolved enabling structure alongside the increasingly isolated central offender.

Chapter 11 — The bank

अध्याय ११ : किनार

With Birju gone, Panikaua possesses digital wealth but lacks the relationships that previously made it usable. Distrust narrows his alternatives. Abhaykant anticipates flight, and an apparently open route across the Kamla-Balan becomes the direction of escape. In the framing conversation, Gop foretells an ending in water and postpones the disclosure of the fugitive’s ordinary name.

The repeated water metaphor now approaches a literal river crossing. The criminal’s seeming freedom depends on a route he has not actually chosen independently. By announcing the danger before the final chapter, the narrative moves suspense away from surprise alone and towards the meaning of the coming loss.

Chapter 12 and epilogue — Disappearance in the river

अध्याय १२ : जलसमाधि

Panikaua crosses the swollen Kamla-Balan in a small boat with a bag containing the remains of his accumulated digital and documentary power. Abhaykant waits on the opposite bank intending a live arrest. The boat overturns; an attempted rescue is stopped by the dangerous current. Debris and the damaged bag return, but no body is recovered. The file treats the disappearance as death, and the ordinary name finally disclosed is Chhotan.

The ending withholds both a courtroom resolution and a recovered body. It also refuses to describe death at greater length merely to intensify spectacle. In the epilogue, Gop rejects the idea that the water has been cleansed: Birju’s knowledge and the smaller participants remain. The two archival chests preserve a warning about recurring methods, while the planned capture’s failure leaves the limits of the investigation visible.
